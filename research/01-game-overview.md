## 1. History: who made it and why

### 1.1 Curve Animation before the game

- Founded **2014** in Ankara by two artists, **Selçuk Yağcı and Ece Bilgehan**, with Turkey's Ministry of
  Science, Industry & Technology "Teknogirişim" support. They were an **animation-for-hire studio**, not a
  game studio: TV series (*İki Balkon* on BluTV Kids; *Fogville Mystery*), clients incl. TRT and the Culture
  Ministry, plus mobile title *Chicken Away*. Sources: https://www.bijetic.com/curve-animation/ , https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/10/21/liars-bar-100k-ccu-steam-curve-animation-turkey-success , https://cdn.fonbulucu.com/archive/filemanager/1763382160_BO85B1D8anMi6eWiIVzIu33WZujSkQHUIS.pdf
- Before Liar's Bar they crowdfunded ~₺12.19M (~$419k) on Fonbulucu for *İki Balkon* (Feb 2024) and publicly
  said the funds would also fund an entry into **games** — Liar's Bar started immediately after.
  Source: https://t24.com.tr/gundem/garaj-modeli-ile-buyume-curve-animasyon-dan-iki-balkon,1151486

### 1.2 Why they made it (their own words, t24 interview, May 2025)

Translated/summarized from the Turkish-language interview (https://t24.com.tr/bilim-teknoloji/en-yenilikci-oynanis-odulu-alan-curve-animasyon-dort-ayda-yaptigimiz-oyunla-buyuk-fark-atarak-kazandik,1241496):

- **The idea came from one employee** — the team then argued it into shape over months. The core pitch:
  combine **the games pirates supposedly played on ships, where the loser dies**, with their animation
  expertise. (Liar's Dice genuinely is the folk game "Perudo/liar's dice" associated with pirates; Liar's
  Deck is the folk card game **Cheat / BS / "I Doubt It"** with a revolver bolted on.)
- **Built in ~4 months**, days and nights, *while still making a film they'd worked on for 5 years*.
- Their theory of why it worked: big companies can't take risks on weird small games; a small team with
  "nothing to lose" can pair **craft (animation) with amateur spirit**.
- It "became an instrument of conversation" on YouTube — a game that *feeds laughter* — and growth was
  **organic**: "we never needed to look for a market." Streamers did their marketing for free.
- By May 2025 the game had been downloaded **~7M times** worldwide (claim) and **toy companies were selling
  figurines of the characters**; the studio is building **three more games at once** (one in the Liar's Bar
  vein). A Nov 2025 investor bulletin claims Liar's Bar briefly hit **#4 on Steam's global best-seller
  list**, 47M+ Steam Award votes, and 2024 company exports > ₺0.5B:
  https://cdn.fonbulucu.com/archive/filemanager/1763382160_BO85B1D8anMi6eWiIVzIu33WZujSkQHUIS.pdf (**UNVERIFIED** marketing claims).

**Takeaway:** no marketing budget, no publisher, no genre experience. The concept + streamability *was* the marketing.

**The VTuber discovery (important for us):** Japanese coverage (GAME Watch, Nov 2024 —
https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/kikaku/1636526.html) documents the killer feature — the game's
**neck/head-turn body language enables full "face-acting" (顔芸) without voice chat**. Sakura Miko ran
listener-participation lobbies where viewers answered personal questions purely by nodding/shaking their pig
heads; a Nijisanji collab produced 4–5 blank roulette pulls before a dramatic first death. Real Sound
(https://realsound.jp/tech/2024/12/post-1857264.html) adds why this streams so well: rules readable at a
glance, turn-based so chat can follow, roulette = pure comedic timing, and viewer-join streams don't require
viewers to be on voice. The category earned a **2024 Vtuber Awards "Stream Game of the Year" nomination**.

**Bugs as marketing:** Know Your Meme's #1 viral artifact was a *bug* — the neck-stretching card peek. The Oct 4
hotfix even closed a real info-leak (spectators seeing cards). Emergent comedy > polish. (Source: KYM, howitspread.)

## 3. The experience: what it actually feels like (and what we must replicate)

### 3.1 The cast

At launch: **Foxy** (gothic red-fox vixen with a murderous-ex backstory), **Scubby** (tattooed Dobermann
"bandit guard dog"), **Toar** (bull/buffalo, rage + loyalty), **Bristle** (pig butcher, widower with a tattoo
of his late wife). Added over time: **Cupcake** the rabbit + **Gerk** the rhino (late Oct 2024, per Flayrah),
**Kudo** the devious red panda (Nov 27, 2024 patch notes), **Ejder** the ex-pit-fighter tiger + **Tusk** the
wild boar — Bristle's brother-in-law, whom Bristle's Table Story implies he murdered (Mar 12, 2025; Flayrah
thread), **Rasim Holloway** and **Dubai** (Jul 2, 2026 patch notes; species **UNVERIFIED**), plus **Riva** —
a non-playable cat singer for the still-unreleased 1v1 "Velvet Room". The store page currently sells "nine
regulars"; the July 2026 patch notes imply 11 playable — the store copy appears stale. Backstories are doled
out in **one-page "Table Stories" comics**: desperate, depressed, psychotic, gambling-addicted losers who
have nothing left to bet but their lives. Sources: https://www.liarsbar.net/g/characters , https://www.flayrah.com/9248/video-game-review-liars-bar , patch notes above, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar%27s_Bar .

### 3.2 Atmosphere & presentation

- **First-person seating at a round table in a dark, seedy sports bar**; your character's paws hold the
  cards; the revolver sits on the table in front of you, the whole game in your field of view.
- Anthropomorphic-animal grimdark ("a grim Zootopia" — Game World Observer) — cute faces doing horrific
  things. Content descriptors: frequent violence/gore, 18+, graphic suicide-adjacent death animations.
- Character voice barks are **AI-generated** (disclosed on the store page); PC Gamer mocked this, players
  didn't care. Distinct bars over time: original neon bar → Arcade Bar (Nov 2024) → Fengdu Bar (Mar 2025,
  Chinese haunted-mansion vibe with "intriguing new regulars") → Soviet train bar "Toska" (Aug 2025, new
  track "Red Snow") → holiday decorations (Dec 2025) → Velvet Room ( teased, unreleased).
- Music per bar, emotes/skins, punky ambient bar noise via the jukebox/music player.

### 3.3 Why it feels dramatic — the emotional anatomy

Synthesizing Forbes, GAME Watch, Real Sound, Flayrah and the rules (rules detail in `99-initial-search-notes.md`):

1. **One-binary-decision turns.** Play 1–3 cards (a declared truth or a lie) OR call "LIAR!". 30-second timer.
   Zero rules dispute potential — a viewer understands the game state in one look (Real Sound's core point).
2. **You're only really playing against one person** — the next player who can call you. That narrows the
   psychology to a personal duel inside a party game. (liarsbar.net strategy pages.)
3. **The slow reveal.** Cards flip face-up, one at a time, on a challenge. All-or-nothing verdict, crowd of
   2–3 spectators watching. BUILT-IN Schadenfreude beat: either the liar squirms or the accuser pays.
4. **The roulette ceremony.** Loser picks up the revolver → cylinder spin → trigger pull → click or BANG.
   Odds *escalate* per loss (1/6 → guaranteed on 6th) and the UI shows the chamber count ("5/6"), turning
   each late pull into a visible countdown to death. Death animation: character blows their own head off /
   slumps across the table; table goes quiet.
5. **The luck cushion.** A caught lie ≠ elimination — you draw a 5/6 chance of survival. Real Sound: this is
   what keeps it a *casual party game* rather than a try-hard strategy game; bold bluffs stay fun, and
   "wrong" calls get laughed off as bad luck rather than betrayal of skill.
6. **Body language as gameplay.** Peer left/right to eye a rival, nod solemnly "I'm telling the truth",
   tremble theatrically. Comedy without voice chat (GAME Watch). This *is* the game's signature social layer.
7. **Rounds stay short; tables stay full.** Fresh 5-card deal every round; matches resolve in ~10–20 minutes;
   the dead spectate and heckle. "Run it back" energy.

**For our clone the irreplaceable beats are: (a) the face-down card slam + verbal-ish claim, (b) the LIAR call
moment, (c) the slow flip, (d) the gun ceremony with escalating visible odds, (e) the comic-gore death, and
(f) expressive puppet bodies at the table.** If our web version nails those six beats, it captures the game;
everything else is chrome.

### 4.3 The genre sibling: Buckshot Roulette validates "lethal gambling" as a category

Buckshot Roulette (Mike Klubnika / Critical Reflex, Godot, made in 2 months, $2.99): 1M copies ~2 weeks after
its Apr 4, 2024 Steam launch; 2M by Jul 2024; 4M by Dec 2024 (~1M of those in the month after the Oct 31, 2024
**4-player multiplayer** update); **6M by Jul 2025; 8M by Dec 2025** — with zero subscription presence.
Sources: https://www.polygon.com/24148258/ , https://www.gamesradar.com/games/horror/he-made-a-viral-horror-game-in-2-months-it-sold-6-million-copies-and-now-he-can-make-whatever-he-wants-for-the-rest-of-his-life-my-final-theory-is-that-gambling-is-very-fun/ ,
https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/buckshot-roulette-sales-8m , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckshot_Roulette .
Klubnika's own theory: "gambling is very fun… it's fun to pick up, and fun to watch over a friend's shoulder

## 5. Lessons: why it went viral, and what's streamable

1. **The concept was the marketing.** A one-line pitch ("Cheat, but the loser plays Russian roulette") with
   an instantly legible dark-comedy table. Studio spent ~$0 on marketing; 113.8k CCU was entirely organic.
2. **Streamability compounds across creator tiers.** megastars (xQc, Sidemen, Markiplier, dunkey) → mid-tier
   gaming YouTubers → VTuber collabs → TikTok/Shorts clip farms. The game generated **clippable 20–40-second
   moments every single round** (the call, the flip, the bang) — the perfect clip-per-hour ratio.
3. **No-voice-chat expressiveness doubled the funnel.** The head-shake "face-acting" let VTubers run
   viewer-participation lobbies — an entire distribution channel (Japan) unavailable to voice-chat-only
   party games. Our product has no built-in voice either — emote/body-language-first design isn't a
   limitation, it's the feature that made the original spread.
4. **Friction floor = price floor.** $6.99 is an impulse buy; the game still hit a 500x-larger audience on
   Roblox being *free*; Buckshot sold 8M at $2.99. Being free + link-share in a browser is the natural
   endpoint of this ladder.
5. **Luck is the friendship preserver.** The roulette cushion (losing a challenge ≠ death) keeps casual
   players bold and post-game chat funny instead of bitter. Pure-skill social deduction (like Coup on BGA)
   never broke out at party scale.
6. **Localize on day 1 (or week 2).** Chinese and Russian were added 9 days in and drove ~73% of the
   lifetime review base (non-English). A web game's locale toggles are nearly free — do EN/TR/zh/RU/ES/PT/DE at
   minimum, Gartic-style.
7. **Live-ops cadence: content updates are the CCU pump.** Every major drop (Chaos, Poker, Slots, Remaster)
   minted a 3–6x CCU spike with ~6 weeks of decay; monthly seasons start Nov 2026. Plan themed bar/cosmetic
   drops on a drumbeat — which is *exactly* our premium-bar-themes monetization model.
8. **Ship details that only streamers will notice, but always fix cheating.** Spectator card-leak fix on day 2;
   a 2-year cheating fight (ban system Aug 2025 → anti-cheat Mar/Nov 2026 → Easy Anti-Cheat pending) cost real
   review score (78% recent vs 89% lifetime). Server-authoritative card state and anti-peek design are
   day-one needs, not v2 features.
9. **The party-game retention curve is fine — and characters carry the brand between sessions.** Median
   lifetime playtime 2h41m; a ~500 CCU floor keeps the game alive and profitable two years later. The
   business is "appointment sessions with friends", not DAU grind (rooms ≫ matchmaking) — and distinctive
   characters generate fan wikis, figurines and fan sites that market for you.

## What this means for our version

- **Attack the empty browser chair.** Verified: no real-time multiplayer Liar's Deck exists on the web; SEO
  farms already farm the query, Roblox's sanitized clone proves demand (66M visits), and the official Android
  app (3.3★, dormant) abandoned the mobile-casual audience. Ship "one link, one room, one revolver" as the
  core slogan-level promise.
- **2–7 players is a real feature, not a compromise.** The original is hard-locked to 4 seats (store page:
  "a table of four"); friend groups of 5–7 are actively unserved. Make larger tables a marketing headline.
  Rebalance the 20-card deck math for 5–7 (more K/Q/A, e.g. a scaled deck) — do not ship 4-player deck math
  to 7 players. (Rules research doc 02 must own the exact scaling.)
- **Rebuild the six emotional beats at web fidelity:** card-slam down with sound; a big "LIAR!" call
  animation; a *slow* one-by-one card flip; a revolver ceremony with a visible chamber counter ("4/6");
  escalating odds across losses; comic-gore-ish death that survives mobile GPUs (silhouette + chair slump +
  table reaction shots — we don't need 3D gore to sell it).
- **Emote-first body language, since we have no built-in voice.** The VTuber case proves head/emote language
  *carries* the social layer when voice is absent: give characters expressive idle animations, nod/shake/
  point/tremble emotes, curious neck-peek animations, and spectator emotes. Treat "no built-in voice chat" as
  our identity, not a gap (Discord on the side stays).
- **Be free, but keep the *authentic* vibe: paid cosmetics instead of pay-to-win.** Premium = cosmetic
  bar themes (neon bar / arcade / Soviet train / haunted Fengdu-style analogues — clearly *inspired, not
  copied*), skins, croupier emotes, maybe seasonal music packs. This mirrors both the community-accepted
  Store (Mar 2025) and genre norms (Buckshot/Gartic prove low price + cosmetics).
- **Playlist-clip cadence:** design the round so that every challenge ends in a screenshot-able / clip-able
  moment; add a compact share/replay summary (who lied, who died, odds at the pull — a "death card").
  Facilitate streamer sessions: spectator slots, slow-mo reveal toggle, room codes, no signup.
- **Operate the drumbeat.** Steam's graph is a live-ops heartbeat: thematic events/updates every 2–6 weeks
  (new bars, seasonal decor like their Dec "New Year decorations", limited modes like "Chaos 2"). Our bar-
  themes-as-DLC gives us exactly that monetization + cadence structure.
- **Day-one integrity: no client-trusted card state.** All hands server-side, no card info sent to
  non-participants (the original shipped this bug on day 1 and hotfixed in 48h — hard lesson). Budget
  anti-cheat/report/mute from launch; their 78% recent rating is the cautionary tale.
- **Localization is cheap and the proven growth lever:** EN + TR + zh-CN + RU first (exactly the original's
  audience), then ES/PT-BR (Alanzoka/Brazil amplifiers), JA (VTuber/colab scene), DE (German press wave).
- **Ship with the "watch over your friend's shoulder" rule-set legibility:** one binary decision per turn,
  visible table card, 30-second turns. The entire streamability research says comprehensibility-at-a-glance
  is why this format went to 100k CCU and Cheat variants on CardzMania never did.
- **Age-gate the darkness safely.** The original's gore/self-harm got it 18+ on Steam and forced Roblox
  clones into potion-land. For ad-network and platform safety on web, stylize the violence (comic, theatrical,
  non-human avatars) and add a content toggle — same drama, fewer ad-policy problems.
- **The mobile browser build is the wedge against the abandoned official Android app.** Its 1.47M installs +
  3.3★ (login failures, crashes) define our QA bar: join-by-code in <10s on 4G, session-reconnect, host
  migration (the original added host migration Nov 2024 — players leave mid-match constantly; plan for it).
